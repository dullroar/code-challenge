title: "An ordinary loop, used in an unusual place"
order: 10
---
The loop is still a loop. What changes is *where* the work happens. In the C#, Java and Go entries the loop has an empty body, and the three clauses of the `for` header do everything. In [[entry:cmake]] a `while()` condition runs a regex, and its captures are the registers. In [[entry:cobol]] the condition is a named fact about the data (`OVER-FORTY`), and `PERFORM ... UNTIL` repeats until it is true. Forth, Lisp and Tcl use their ordinary loop words. Read these beside the others: they show what stays the same when the loop is honest. [[entry:javascript]] is the original's bodiless `for` in Node.js: three clauses, an object literal for state, and a timer for the animation.
